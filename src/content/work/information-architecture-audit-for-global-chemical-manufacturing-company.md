## Starting point

A business unit of a global chemical manufacturing company was developing updated sales concepts for the UK and Ireland. Before committing to new digital experiences, the organisation needed to understand whether the underlying information architecture could support them.

Relevant customer, product and sales information crossed existing content, business and ERP systems. Differences in structure, ownership and integration could affect what customers and sales teams were able to find, understand and use.

## The decision to make

The organisation needed to determine which sales concepts were feasible with the current information and system landscape, where information or integration gaps would limit the experience, and which improvements should be prioritised before implementation.

## How we worked

- Reviewed the proposed sales concepts, customer journeys and the information required at important touchpoints.
- Traced relevant information to its source systems and identified how it moved into customer- and sales-facing channels.
- Audited information structure, findability, consistency, reuse and ownership across the relevant landscape.
- Examined ERP, API and integration dependencies affecting the proposed experiences.
- Identified missing information, duplicated responsibilities and areas where local and global structures did not align.
- Translated the findings into prioritised recommendations covering information, content, data, integration and governance.

## What the client gained

The audit gave the business unit an evidence-based view of the information and architectural conditions behind its sales concepts. It made dependencies and risks visible before implementation and clarified which improvements were necessary to support a consistent digital sales experience.

The result was a more informed basis for prioritising the concepts and coordinating subsequent work across business, content, data, ERP and technology stakeholders.
