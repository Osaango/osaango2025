## Starting point

A growing international organisation was transforming digital services that depended on several platforms, partners and legacy systems. Individual initiatives could make local progress, but without a shared integration direction they risked introducing new point-to-point dependencies and inconsistent responsibilities.

The organisation needed an architecture that supported current transformation work while remaining usable for future services and partners.

## The decision to make

The key question was how information and responsibilities should cross domain and organisational boundaries. The target architecture needed to clarify what should be exposed through reusable APIs, where events or other integration styles were appropriate, and how legacy constraints could be managed during transition.

## How we worked

- Connected business capabilities and transformation initiatives to their integration and information needs.
- Reviewed the current architecture, important information flows, system responsibilities and external dependencies.
- Defined domain boundaries and principles for selecting API, event, batch and other integration patterns.
- Made expectations for ownership, security, data quality, observability and lifecycle management explicit.
- Evaluated where shared platform capabilities could reduce duplication without centralising every implementation decision.
- Facilitated decisions among service owners, architects, delivery teams, partners and vendors.
- Developed a transition direction linking near-term delivery choices with the longer-term target architecture.

## What the client gained

The engagement produced a target integration architecture, clearer interface responsibilities, reusable decision principles and a transition path for moving from the current landscape toward the intended platform direction.

This gave business and technology stakeholders a common basis for evaluating future integration decisions instead of solving each initiative as an isolated project.
