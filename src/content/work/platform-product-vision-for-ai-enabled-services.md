## Starting point

A global SaaS organisation needed a clearer product vision for its platform. Customer and partner integrations were becoming increasingly strategic, while AI and automation created many possible directions for the roadmap.

The opportunity was significant, but the boundary between shared platform capabilities, product-specific features and partner solutions was not sufficiently clear. Ownership and prioritisation needed the same attention as technology.

## The decision to make

The leadership team needed to decide which reusable capabilities the platform should provide, where AI could improve a meaningful customer or operational outcome, and how product, architecture and governance decisions should be sequenced.

## How we worked

- Clarified customer, partner and internal-team needs around integrations and platform capabilities.
- Distinguished requested features from the underlying jobs, outcomes and recurring capability needs.
- Mapped the customer-visible, service-logic and infrastructure layers involved in the most important opportunities.
- Evaluated AI and automation opportunities against expected value, data readiness, architectural fit, risk, adoption and operational ownership.
- Clarified the boundaries between platform, product, domain-team and partner responsibilities.
- Connected product strategy, API and integration architecture, governance and operating-model decisions.
- Built prioritisation criteria and a sequenced roadmap for capability development and validation.

## What the client gained

The leadership team gained a clearer platform value proposition, a more explicit product boundary and a prioritised set of capability and AI hypotheses. The roadmap connected investment decisions with the evidence, architecture, ownership and governance required to move from an idea to a dependable service.

This made it easier to evaluate new AI or integration requests according to their contribution to the platform strategy rather than their novelty alone.
